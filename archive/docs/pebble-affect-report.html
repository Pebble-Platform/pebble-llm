Speech & Text Affect Models — Progress Report

Backbone selection for the voice modality, and an MLM-adaptation ablation for the text modality — both decided on evidence from rigorous 3-seed evaluations.
Date: 18 June 2026 Scope: Decision Engine — pre-generation affect scoring Compute: Kaggle P100 GPU (headless) Status: Both experiments complete

Executive summary

  • Mission. Read a user's emotional state before responding, so someone venting in distress is met with support — not a blanket safety refusal triggered by their words.
  • Voice backbone selected: WavLM-Large. It beat the emotion-specialised encoder (emotion2vec) on every seed — emotion macro-F1 0.609 vs 0.537 (+13%) and higher distress precision. A live demo website is up.
  • Text MLM adaptation: not worth shipping. Adapting the encoder on in-domain text gave a tiny emotion gain (+0.013 F1) but measurably hurt severity (−0.045) and calibration. We ship the simpler vanilla model (MLM-off).
  • Common theme: in both modalities the stronger/simpler general model won. We avoided a more expensive path that the data did not justify.
  • Delivered: a runnable voice demo site, a one-command Kaggle job runner, reproducible sample tooling, and full documentation.

1Background — why this project exists

Pebble is built for people reaching out while under stress. The founding observation was a failure mode of general-purpose assistants.

A user in distress was refused by a general AI assistant because their message contained profanity — language that was a symptom of how upset they were, not abuse. A safety filter that reacts to surface words can shut out exactly the person who most needs help.

Pebble's answer is to understand affect before it responds. A lightweight classifier scores emotion and distress / severity ahead of generation, so the Decision Engine can tell "someone hurting, venting in strong words" apart from a genuine policy violation — and route the first to care instead of a refusal. Getting that distinction right is the core reason these models exist.

2Approach & why these models

We score affect with small, self-hosted models that run before generation and emit structured numbers — not a second generative LLM.

  • Self-hosted, not an external API. Sensitive mental-health text stays in-house; cost is predictable, latency is low, and there's no hard dependency on a third party (a hosted model is only the backup path).
  • A classifier, not a generator. We need fast, typed scores (emotion, severity, safety) — an encoder plus small heads delivers that cheaply, where a generative model would be slower and costlier.

Text → NeoBERT

A modern, compact BERT-style encoder. Strong language representations at a fraction of an LLM's size, easy to self-host and fine-tune into typed heads (emotion + severity). Chosen over a generative LLM for speed, cost, and privacy.

Voice → frozen encoder + light heads

We freeze a pretrained speech model and train only small heads (a "linear probe"), which makes backbone selection cheap and fast. Candidates: WavLM-Large (a strong general self-supervised speech model) vs emotion2vec (specialised for emotion) — picked on evidence (§4) before any expensive full fine-tune.

Choosing it now avoids building the heavier emotion2vec dependency (FunASR/ModelScope) for no benefit.

5Text modality — does in-domain MLM adaptation help?

The text model fine-tunes NeoBERT into two heads: emotion (28 classes) and severity (0–1 regression). The question: if we first adapt the encoder on a large, separate in-domain corpus via masked-language modelling (the standard TAPT/DAPT technique), do downstream results improve? We ran both arms — adapted (MLM-on) vs vanilla (MLM-off) — across 3 seeds for a paired delta.

Decision & rationale. Ship the vanilla MLM-off model. MLM adaptation buys a small, consistent emotion gain but at a real cost to severity accuracy and calibration — and for Pebble, severity and trustworthy confidence matter more than a few F1 points (they sit behind safety gates). The extra MLM pre-training stage is therefore not justified for this multi-task setup.
Reading the absolute numbers. Emotion F1 ≈ 0.31 looks modest, but this was a deliberately cheap ablation (2,500 examples/task, 3 epochs) built to compare the two arms, not to maximise quality. For context: random guessing on 28 classes ≈ 0.036, and a fully-trained BERT baseline in the literature ≈ 0.46. Severity correlation ≈ 0.61 and MAE ≈ 0.13 are already in a healthy range. Production quality comes from scaling data + epochs, which is a separate step.

7Recommended next steps

  • Voice: proceed with WavLM-Large; scale the training data beyond RAVDESS and tune the distress threshold on Pebble's own audio before any deployment.
  • Text: drop the MLM-adaptation stage; invest the budget in scaling labelled data + epochs to lift emotion F1 toward the ~0.46 reference, while protecting severity/calibration.
  • Both: run the formal pre-deployment evaluation against the safety gates (distress recall, severity-band MAE) before shipping to the Decision Engine.

8Notable prior work reviewed

Both experiments are grounded in a 41-paper deep-read research library (text + speech). The most influential, grouped by theme:

Encoders & backbones we build on

  • WavLM: Large-Scale Self-Supervised Learning for Full-Stack Speech Processing — Chen et al., IEEE JSTSP 2022. → the voice backbone we selected.
  • emotion2vec: Self-Supervised Pre-Training for Speech Emotion Representation — Ma et al., ACL Findings 2024. → the emotion-specialised candidate we benchmarked against.
  • ModernBERT: A Modern Bidirectional Encoder — Warner et al., 2024. → the modern-encoder lineage behind our NeoBERT text backbone.

Multi-task learning (our two-head emotion + severity design)

  • Multi-Task Learning Using Uncertainty to Weigh Losses — Kendall, Gal & Cipolla, CVPR 2018. → principled way to balance the emotion and severity losses.
  • GradNorm: Gradient Normalization for Adaptive Loss Balancing — Chen et al., ICML 2018.
  • Gradient Surgery for Multi-Task Learning (PCGrad) — Yu et al., NeurIPS 2020. → resolves conflicting gradients between tasks.

Mental-health & crisis-aware NLP (the mission)

  • FAIIR — crisis-line multi-label classification, npj Digital Medicine 2025. → closest deployed system to Pebble's intent.
  • MentalBERT: Pretrained Language Models for Mental Healthcare — Ji et al., LREC 2022. → domain-pretrained encoder for mental-health text.
  • Emo Pillars — LLM-teacher distillation on GoEmotions, ACL Findings 2025. → our emotion dataset + a distillation recipe.
  • A Computational Approach to Understanding Empathy in Text-Based Mental Health Support — Sharma et al., EMNLP 2020.

Speech emotion & voice mental-health markers

  • Survey of Deep Representation Learning for Speech Emotion Recognition — Latif et al., IEEE T-AFFC 2023.
  • MMER: Multimodal Multi-task Learning for Speech Emotion Recognition — Ghosh et al., Interspeech 2023.
  • Speech Emotion Recognition using Self-Supervised Features — Morais et al., ICASSP 2022. → the frozen-SSL-probe approach our voice pilot uses.
  • SER in Mental Health: A Systematic Review of Voice-Based Applications — Jordan et al., JMIR Mental Health 2025.
