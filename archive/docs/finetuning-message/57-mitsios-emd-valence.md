## Summary
Reframes discrete text-emotion classification (on GoEmotions, ISEAR, WASSA-21) as **ordinal** classification. Emotions are first arranged in a 1D sequence by **valence** level; the model is then extended to a **2D ordinal** scheme by adding a second classifier head for **arousal**, with valence and arousal heads trained jointly via a combined loss. Backbone is a **RoBERTa-CNN**. The ordinal formulation keeps accuracy and F1 essentially at the baseline RoBERTa-CNN level, but **changes the error distribution**: misclassifications concentrate at valence-distance 1 (adjacent emotions) instead of being spread across distance >3 — i.e., far fewer **severe, cross-valence** errors (predicting a positive emotion on a negative-valence input or vice versa).

## Best point — Method to adopt
**Order the GoEmotions taxonomy along the valence axis and train the emotion head with a distance-aware (ordinal) loss, so the discrete emotion prediction is penalized more for crossing valence than for an adjacent slip — making the emotion head coherent with the continuous valence head "for free."** The paper shows this preserves accuracy/F1 while collapsing severe cross-valence errors into adjacent ones.
- **How to apply to Pebble:** Map Pebble's 12-label emotion taxonomy to valence ranks, then replace (or augment) the flat softmax CE on the emotion head with a distance-aware objective — **the CORAL/ordinal machinery R2 already implements (paper 48) ported from the severity head to the emotion head**, or a simpler soft-label/EMD-style penalty over valence-ordered classes. Add a **cross-valence error rate** (% of emotion errors that flip valence sign) as a reported metric and a coherence check against the continuous valence head. Expected payoff: an emotion head whose mistakes stay on the right side of the valence axis, which matters disproportionately for a mental-health companion (mislabeling a distressed message as a positive emotion is the costly error).

## ▶ Relation to existing repo machinery
- **Ordinal lineage already in-repo.** `r2-suicide-risk-dualhead.py` implements CORAL (single shared weight + ordered biases) for *severity* (paper 48). This paper is the same idea applied to *emotion-by-valence*. The transfer is mechanical: the emotion taxonomy needs a valence ordering (SU<…< for severity becomes most-negative<…<most-positive for emotion), after which the same K−1-threshold CORAL loss — or a distance-weighted CE/EMD — applies to the emotion head.
- **Two realization choices for the distance-aware loss** (ablate, don't assume): (a) **CORAL/ordinal-regression** on valence-ranked emotion classes (rank-monotone, reuses R2 code); (b) **EMD / soft-label over valence-ordered bins** (the framing the caller expected; note the paper itself does NOT use EMD — see Caveats). Both penalize cross-valence errors more than adjacent ones; CORAL is the lower-effort path given existing code.

## Dataset
GoEmotions (23 of 27 emotions used), ISEAR (~7.6K sentences), WASSA-21. All on Pebble's planned emotion-transfer source list (GoEmotions is the warm-start corpus; WASSA is intensity-adjacent). No new acquisition required — this is a loss/label-ordering technique applied to corpora Pebble already targets.

## Caveats
- **Loss-function premise correction (important).** The task framing supplied to this analysis described an **Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) loss**. Two independent secondary summaries of the paper state the ordinal training uses **MSE on ordinal targets**, and neither the ACL abstract nor the arXiv abstract mentions EMD. **The "EMD loss" attribution appears to be incorrect for this paper** — the actual mechanism is ordinal-target regression (MSE) over valence-ordered classes, not EMD. The *transferable idea* (valence-order the labels + distance-aware loss to cut cross-valence errors) is unaffected; only the specific loss name is. Treat EMD as one *candidate* realization for Pebble, not as this paper's method. Flagged rather than fabricated.
- **No exact numbers verified.** The full PDF text was not machine-readable in this pass (PDF returned as binary; arXiv HTML mirror returned the LaTeX template). Accuracy/F1 ≈ baseline and the "errors shift from distance >3 to distance 1" finding are confirmed *qualitatively* across the ACL page, arXiv abstract, and two secondary reviews, but the precise tables (per-distance error percentages, exact accuracy/F1) are **not quoted here** — confidence on the headline mechanism is high; on exact magnitudes, medium.
- **Domain mismatch.** GoEmotions/ISEAR/WASSA are general-emotion, not mental-health/crisis text (D2=0). The valence-ordering technique transfers; the *label set and register* do not — Pebble must define its own valence ordering over its 12-label taxonomy and validate on its own (Gemini/Protocol) data.
- **Backbone is RoBERTa-CNN, not a plain encoder + linear head.** The CNN-over-RoBERTa feature extractor is an architectural detail Pebble need not copy; the head-side ordinal reformulation is the portable part.
