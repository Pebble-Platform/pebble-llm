## 2. Problem motivation

AVEC 2016 is a benchmark challenge — its purpose is to give the depression and emotion-recognition communities a common, strictly-comparable test bed for audio/visual/physiological methods. Two sub-challenges run in parallel:

- **Depression Classification Sub-Challenge (DCC):** classify a person as depressed or not, where the binary ground truth is derived from the self-reported **PHQ-8** score per human-agent interview. Primary metric: average F1 over the two classes (depressed / not depressed). Participants are *also* encouraged to predict continuous PHQ-8 severity and report RMSE (plus accuracy, precision, recall).
- **Multimodal Affect Recognition Sub-Challenge (MASC):** fully continuous regression of two affect dimensions — **Arousal** and **Valence** — predicted at every frame. Primary metric: **Concordance Correlation Coefficient (CCC)**.

The deeper motivation (Introduction) is to push emotion recognition toward "behaviomedical systems… able to deal with large volumes of non-prototypical naturalistic behaviour" — exactly the messy, real-world signal a diagnostic or monitoring tool must face. This is the canonical, peer-reviewed root of the **speech-based depression-severity-from-voice** task that Pebble's voice-message modality inherits.

## 3. Position in the literature

AVEC 2016 is the sixth event in the AVEC series. The emotion sub-challenge is "a refined re-run of the AVEC 2015 challenge," reusing the RECOLA corpus. The depression sub-challenge "sees the return of depression analysis," last seen in AVEC 2013 and 2014 — but on a **new** corpus, the DAIC-WOZ. The lineage matters: AVEC defined the feature sets (COVAREP, then GeMAPS/eGeMAPS) and the metrics (RMSE/MAE for severity, CCC for continuous affect) that the entire downstream speech-depression literature (papers 38/39/40 in this set, and the wav2vec2/SSL-era work) is still benchmarked against.

## 4. Dataset deep dive

### 4.1 DAIC-WOZ (depression corpus)

The **Distress Analysis Interview Corpus — Wizard-of-Oz (DAIC-WOZ)** is the depression sub-challenge corpus, a part of the larger DAIC (Gratch et al., LREC 2014). It contains clinical interviews "designed to support the diagnosis of psychological distress conditions such as anxiety, depression, and post-traumatic stress disorder." Interviews are conducted by an **animated virtual interviewer called Ellie**, controlled by a human "wizard" in another room. Each session yields audio + video recordings + questionnaire responses; data is transcribed and annotated for verbal/non-verbal features. **For ethical reasons, no raw video is released** — only derived features. Audio and transcripts *are* shared. Data is freely available for research at `http://dcapswoz.ict.usc.edu`.

- **Total sessions: 189** (the paper does not print this number; it is the canonical DAIC-WOZ AVEC-2016 release count — ✔ corroborated, see Source-access note).
- **Official AVEC 2016 partition (✔ corroborated):** train **107**, development **35**, test **47**. Class balance: train 77 depressed / 30 not-depressed; dev 23/12; test 33/14. The challenge released train+dev labels publicly and withheld the test labels for blind scoring.

### 4.2 Depression labels (PHQ-8)

Depression is labelled with a **single value per recording** using the self-assessed **PHQ-8** questionnaire — "similar to the PHQ-9 questionnaire, but with the suicidal ideation question removed for ethical reasons." (§2.1) PHQ-8 ranges 0–24.

- **Mean depression severity on train+dev: M = 6.67, SD = 5.75** (§2.1, ✔ corroborated).
- **Mean-predictor baseline** (constantly predicting the mean): **RMSE = 5.73, MAE = 4.74** (§2.1, ✔ corroborated). This is the floor any severity model must beat — and, importantly, *the audio/video baselines do NOT beat it* (see §6).

### 4.3 RECOLA (emotion corpus)

The **Remote Collaborative and Affective Interactions (RECOLA)** database underlies the MASC emotion sub-challenge. Multimodal signals — audio, video, ECG, EDA — were synchronously recorded from **27 French-speaking subjects** (different nationalities: French, Italian, German) during a remote collaborative task. Time-continuous arousal/valence ratings (40 ms binned frames) were produced by **six gender-balanced raters** for the first five minutes of each recording. Inter-rater reliability is very strong (Table 1): normalised CCC ≈ .431 arousal / .478 valence, ICC ≈ .827 / .844. Partition (Table 2): **train 9, dev 9, test 9** subjects, stratified on gender and mother tongue. Data is freely available at `http://diuf.unifr.ch/diva/recola`.

## 5. Method (baseline systems)

Deliberately simple and open-source, "refraining from feature-space optimisation and optimising on test data" for reproducibility (§5).

### 5.1 Audio feature sets — the load-bearing part for Pebble

- **Depression / DAIC-WOZ → COVAREP (v1.3.2):** an open-source Matlab/Octave toolbox capturing **voice quality + prosody**, features "shown to be correlated with psychological distress and depression" (§2.2.2). Extracted LLDs:
  - *Prosodic:* fundamental frequency (F0), voicing (VUV).
  - *Voice quality:* NAQ, QOQ, H1H2, PSP, MDQ, peakSlope, Rd (glottal-source parameters).
  - *Spectral:* Mel cepstral coefficients (MCEP 0–24), Harmonic Model & Phase Distortion mean (HMPDM 0–24) and deviations (HMPDD 0–12).
  - Sampled frame-wise at **100 Hz**.
- **Emotion / RECOLA → eGeMAPS (extended Geneva Minimalistic Acoustic Parameter Set):** **88 acoustic features** (§3.2.2, ✔ corroborated), an expert-knowledge minimalistic set covering spectral, cepstral, prosodic, and voice-quality information, extracted with **openSMILE**. Functionals (arithmetic mean + coefficient of variation) over 42 LLDs, plus percentiles, slope statistics, and 6 temporal features (loudness-peak rate, voiced/unvoiced segment durations, pseudo-syllable rate). Extracted over overlapping windows shifted at **40 ms**.

### 5.2 Depression baseline models

- **Classification:** linear SVM with SGD (scikit-learn), frame-wise (100 Hz audio / 30 Hz video), temporal fusion by **majority voting** over all frames of the interview. Grid-searched over loss {log, hinge}, regularization {L1, L2}, α {1e1…1e-5}. Audio-video ensemble via logical **AND**.
- **Severity regression:** **random-forest regressor** (best = 10 trees), frame-wise, interview-level prediction by **averaging** frame outputs; audio+video fusion by averaging the two unimodal regressor outputs.

### 5.3 Emotion baseline model

Linear SVM regression (liblinear, L2-regularised L2-loss), per modality, with grid-searched window size W (4–14 s) and gold-standard delay D (0–8 s, to compensate rater reaction time). Late fusion of up to 8 modalities by **linear regression** of unimodal predictions (Weka). Key finding on windows: **valence needs longer windows and longer delay than arousal** (mean W̄_A = 5.3 s, W̄_V = 9.3 s; D̄_A = 1.2 s, D̄_V = 1.8 s) (§4.2).

## 6. Experiments and results

### 6.1 Depression classification (Table 3, F1; "not depressed" in brackets)

| Partition | Modality | F1 | Precision | Recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dev | Audio | .462 (.682) | .316 (.938) | .857 (.54) |
| Dev | Video | .500 (.896) | .600 (.867) | .428 (.928) |
| Dev | Ensemble | .500 (.896) | .600 (.867) | .428 (.928) |
| Test | Audio | .410 (.582) | .267 (.941) | .889 (.421) |
| Test | Video | .583 (.851) | .467 (.938) | .778 (.790) |
| Test | Ensemble | .583 (.857) | .467 (.938) | .778 (.790) |

(✔ corroborated.) Note: **audio-only has the highest recall on the depressed class** (test recall .889) but low precision (.267) — a recall-first profile. The AND-ensemble collapses to video because video positives are a subset of audio positives.

### 6.2 Depression severity regression (Table 4, RMSE / MAE on PHQ-8)

| Partition | Modality | RMSE | MAE |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dev | Audio | 6.74 | 5.36 |
| Dev | Video | 7.13 | 5.88 |
| Dev | Audio-Video | 6.62 | 5.52 |
| Test | Audio | **7.78** | 5.72 |
| Test | Video | **6.97** | 6.12 |
| Test | Audio-Video | 7.05 | 5.66 |

(✔ corroborated against published version.) **Crucial caveat the paper does not flag loudly:** *none of these beat the mean-predictor floor of RMSE 5.73 / MAE 4.74.* On the test set, audio-only RMSE (7.78) is **worse** than constant-mean prediction. This is a sobering existence-proof that **PHQ-8 severity from acoustic features alone is a very hard regression** on a 189-session corpus.

### 6.3 Emotion / affect recognition (Table 6, CCC)

| Modality | Arousal (Dev / Test) | Valence (Dev / Test) |
|---|---|---|
| Audio | .796 / **.648** | .455 / .375 |
| Video-appearance | .483 / .343 | .474 / .486 |
| Video-geometric | .379 / .272 | .612 / .507 |
| ECG | .271 / .158 | .153 / .121 |
| HRHRV | .379 / .334 | .293 / .198 |
| EDA | .073 / .075 | .194 / .228 |
| **Multimodal (late fusion)** | **.821 / .683** | **.683 / .639** |

(✔ corroborated.) Headline findings: **audio dominates arousal** (test CCC .648 audio vs .343 video-appearance); **video dominates valence** (test .507 geometric vs .375 audio). **Multimodal fusion wins everywhere** (test arousal .683, valence .639). Even modalities that are weak alone (SCR for arousal, SCL for valence) *contribute* in fusion (Figure 2 contribution percentages).

## 7. Key takeaway profile

- The **audio modality carries depression-recall and arousal**, video carries valence — a clean modality-division-of-labour that recurs throughout the speech-affect literature.
- **Severity regression from voice is hard**: the strongest unimodal audio RMSE on test (7.78) does not beat a constant baseline (5.73). This is the realistic prior for any "PHQ-from-voice" head.
- **Fusion helps emotion more than it helps depression** here: emotion multimodal CCC clearly beats every unimodal score; depression AV-fusion only marginally beats unimodal and still loses to the mean predictor.

### What the paper actually does

AVEC 2016 defines two reproducible benchmark tasks on two corpora and reports deliberately-simple open-source baselines:

- **Depression (DAIC-WOZ, 189 Ellie interviews, PHQ-8 labels).** Binary classification (depressed/not, threshold on PHQ-8) with a linear SGD-SVM, frame-wise at 100 Hz, interview-level by majority vote; plus severity regression with a 10-tree random forest, interview-level by frame-averaging. Audio features = **COVAREP** (F0/VUV prosody; NAQ/QOQ/H1H2/PSP/MDQ/peakSlope/Rd voice-quality; MCEP 0–24 + HMPDM/HMPDD spectral). Best test results: classification F1 .583 (video/ensemble), audio recall .889; severity test RMSE audio **7.78** / video **6.97** / AV 7.05, MAE 5.66–6.12 (Table 4) — **none beating the mean-predictor floor 5.73/4.74**.
- **Emotion (RECOLA, 27 French speakers, continuous arousal/valence).** Linear-SVM regression per modality with optimised window W and delay D, late-fused by linear regression. Audio = **eGeMAPS (88 features)**. Best test CCC: audio arousal **.648**, video-geometric valence **.507**, **multimodal arousal .683 / valence .639** (Table 6).

### Parts directly useful for Pebble

1. **PHQ-8 self-report severity as the canonical voice→depression target, with a published RMSE/MAE floor (5.73 / 4.74) and a "best audio" test RMSE of 7.78** (§2.1, Table 4). → **D-D, D-C.** This is the literature anchor for Pebble's voice-message severity head: the metric (RMSE/MAE on a 0–24 self-report scale, plus Pearson per D-D) and the transfer source (DAIC-WOZ) both originate here. *Transfer risk:* high. DAIC-WOZ is adult clinical-interview English speech, 189 sessions; the absolute RMSE numbers will not hold for child voice messages and the corpus is too small to fine-tune NeoBERT-scale models on alone — use it as a *calibration anchor / external eval*, not a training set.
2. **eGeMAPS (88-dim) and COVAREP as the standardised, openSMILE-extractable acoustic feature vectors** (§2.2.2, §3.2.2). → **D-D, D-H.** If Pebble adds any voice-message modality, eGeMAPS is the minimal, reproducible, license-clean baseline feature set that every comparison paper uses; it is a concrete substitute/anchor for D-H and the natural baseline to beat before reaching for SSL embeddings. *Transfer risk:* moderate. eGeMAPS was validated on adult speech; child fundamental-frequency ranges differ, so per-speaker (per-child) standardisation — which the paper found best for physiology — is the likely fix.
3. **CCC as the metric for continuous affect, and the audio↔arousal / video↔valence division** (§4.2, Table 6). → **D-D, D-A.** Pebble's `emotion`/`severity` heads can borrow CCC (or its Pearson component) for any continuous-affect output and should *expect audio to carry arousal-like activation but be weak on valence-like pleasantness* — meaning a text-encoder (NeoBERT) likely complements voice precisely where voice is weakest (valence/semantics). This is direct evidence for a **text-primary, voice-as-complement** architecture rather than voice-only.
4. **Per-speaker standardisation beat global standardisation for the noisiest signals** (§4.2). → **D-D, D-G.** For a child-facing, per-user product, normalising acoustic features per child (running mean/var) is the paper's empirically-best recipe for the hardest signals — a concrete config for any Pebble voice head and a calibration-policy hook (D-G).
5. **Interview-level aggregation by frame averaging / majority vote** (§4.1). → **D-C.** Turn-level-then-aggregate is exactly Pebble's mid-conversation→session shape; the paper's simple averaging baseline is the trivial aggregator to beat with attention pooling.

### How each part helps Pebble succeed

- **Severity head (D-D / D-C).** Adopt PHQ-8-style 0–24 self-report severity as the *external benchmark* for the voice/severity head and report RMSE + MAE + Pearson against DAIC-WOZ as a held-out calibration anchor — never as the only training data. Set realistic expectations in the Pebble paper: AVEC 2016's best audio test RMSE (7.78) is *worse than predicting the mean* (5.73), so a Pebble severity head that merely correlates (Pearson > 0) with PHQ-8-like labels is already at-or-above the 2016 acoustic state of practice. Use the 5.73/4.74 floor as the "must-beat" line in any severity ablation table.
- **Voice feature config (D-D / D-H).** Wire openSMILE eGeMAPS (88-dim) as the v1 acoustic baseline for any voice-message experiment, with **per-child** standardisation. This is cheap, reproducible, and gives a defensible baseline before introducing SSL audio embeddings (papers 39/40) — the same ordering AVEC used (eGeMAPS baseline → later SSL challengers).
- **Architecture decision (D-A).** Cite the audio↔arousal / video(text)↔valence split as the reason Pebble keeps NeoBERT (text) as the primary backbone: voice contributes activation/distress-intensity signal, text contributes semantic valence. The fusion result (multimodal > unimodal everywhere on emotion) supports adding voice as a *late-fused complement* to NeoBERT rather than replacing it.
- **Aggregation (D-C).** Implement turn-level scoring then session-level aggregation; start from AVEC's averaging baseline and treat any learned pooling as the improvement to measure.
- **Calibration (D-G).** Per-speaker standardisation maps directly onto Pebble's per-user calibration: maintain a running per-child acoustic baseline and normalise incoming voice features against it before the severity head.

### Child mental-health lens

- **Transfer validity is the central caveat.** DAIC-WOZ is **adult** (mean age unstated but a clinical-screening population), **English**, **clinical-interview** speech with a virtual interviewer — three steps removed from Pebble's child, multilingual, spontaneous voice-message register. The acoustic correlates of depression that COVAREP captures (reduced vowel space, prosodic flattening; refs 32/33) are validated on adults; **whether they hold for children's voices is unestablished** and a genuine open research question Pebble could own.
- **PHQ-8 deliberately removes the suicidal-ideation item "for ethical reasons."** This is directly aligned with Pebble's v1 decision to **not** train a learned safety head — the field's canonical depression-severity instrument itself carves suicide risk out of the severity scale and treats it separately. Pebble's severity head measuring distress *intensity* while safety is handled by a separate (heuristic, in v1) pathway mirrors PHQ-8's own design ethics. Cite this as precedent.
- **Self-report labels, not clinician diagnosis.** PHQ-8 is self-assessed — a "silver-ish" label by Pebble's own taxonomy (reused public labels, not expert per-instance annotation). This makes DAIC-WOZ a *fair* analogue for Pebble's silver-label regime: the canonical voice-depression benchmark itself is built on self-report, so Pebble's reuse of dataset labels is not a unique compromise.
- **No raw video, controlled-access data.** AVEC withheld raw video and released only derived features under a research-access agreement — the governance pattern Pebble must adopt for any child voice data (release features/embeddings, never raw audio; controlled access).
- **Tiny corpus → over-claiming risk.** 189 sessions / 47 test sessions is small; "Most DAIC-WOZ depression classifiers are invalid… don't learn task-specific features" (a 2025 reproducibility study surfaced in search) is a live warning. Pebble must not fine-tune large models on DAIC-WOZ alone and must report variance, not just point RMSE.

### Limitations & open questions for Pebble

- **Contradiction / gap vs Pebble's plan (and vs the text-only papers in this set):** AVEC 2016 is the strongest evidence that **acoustic-only depression severity does not beat a constant baseline** (test audio RMSE 7.78 > mean-predictor 5.73). This *contradicts* any implicit assumption that adding a voice modality automatically improves a severity head — on this canonical corpus it made severity *worse* than predicting the mean, while it clearly helped *emotion* (CCC). Implication for Pebble: voice is a defensible complement for **emotion/arousal**, but for **severity** the text backbone (NeoBERT, per D-A) is likely to remain the stronger signal, and voice should be added only with an ablation that proves it beats the mean-predictor floor. This is a direct tension with the "voice-message modality as the canonical mental-health head" framing — the canonical task exists, but its 2016 baseline is *negative* for severity.
- **Gap vs the SSL papers (39/40):** AVEC 2016's baselines predate wav2vec2/WavLM; its RMSE numbers are the *floor* the SSL-era papers improved on. Pebble should benchmark against the modern SSL results, not the 2016 baselines, while still citing 2016 for the task definition, metric, and the mean-predictor floor.
- **Corpus size and population mismatch:** 189 adult clinical-interview sessions cannot validate child-register transfer. Open question: is there *any* child/adolescent voice-depression corpus with PHQ-8-style labels? If not, Pebble's child-register voice calibration slice would be a first-of-its-kind contribution.
- **No turn-level severity in the source task.** AVEC scores at the *interview* level (one PHQ-8 per session); Pebble needs *turn-level / mid-conversation* scoring. The averaging aggregation here gives no guidance on how severity distributes within a session — a modelling gap Pebble must fill and cannot borrow.
- **Self-report ceiling.** PHQ-8 is the label *and* the ceiling; a model can at best predict what the person reported about themselves, which for children (limited interoception, social-desirability) may diverge from observable distress more than for the adult DAIC-WOZ population.
