## 2. One-line thesis for Pebble

AVEC 2019 is the canonical, peer-reviewed benchmark for **depression-severity estimation from the speech of someone talking to an autonomous AI interviewer** (the E-DAIC "Ellie" agent, AI-driven test partition). For Pebble's **voice-message modality** — analyzing a child's recorded voice messages to estimate distress/depression — it is the closest published analogue to "predict a clinical severity score from free-form spoken audio to a machine," and it supplies the metric (CCC), the standard audio feature stacks (eGeMAPS, MFCC, DeepSpectrum, BoAW), and the hard empirical fact that going from a human-driven (WoZ) to a fully autonomous AI interviewer **degrades** depression-detection accuracy.

## 3. The three sub-challenges (overview)

AVEC 2019 bundled three regression tasks, all ranked by Concordance Correlation Coefficient (CCC), each contributor limited to five test-set submissions:

1. **State-of-Mind Sub-challenge (SoMS)** — predict self-reported mood (10-point Likert, arousal/valence) after telling two positive and two negative personal narratives (5 min each); USoM (Ulm State-of-Mind) corpus, 111 subjects, 34h23m.
2. **Detecting Depression with AI Sub-challenge (DDS)** — regress PHQ-8 depression severity from audiovisual recordings of a clinical interview conducted by a virtual agent; **E-DAIC** corpus, 275 subjects, 73h10m. **This is the Pebble-relevant task.**
3. **Cross-cultural Emotion Sub-challenge (CES)** — predict time-continuous arousal/valence/liking on Chinese speakers using only German+Hungarian training data; SEWA corpus, 200 subjects, 8h28m.

## 4. The DDS task and E-DAIC corpus (deep dive)

**Corpus.** The Extended Distress Analysis Interview Corpus (E-DAIC) is an extended version of WOZ-DAIC. It contains semi-clinical interviews "designed to support the diagnosis of psychological distress conditions such as anxiety, depression, and post-traumatic stress disorder," conducted by an animated virtual interviewer named **Ellie**. Data include audio, video, **automatically transcribed text via Google Cloud speech recognition**, and questionnaire responses. The population is US military veterans / general population recruited for the SimSensei effort (DeVault et al. 2014).

**The autonomous-agent design (the central novelty).** In the *WoZ* interviews, Ellie is remotely puppeted by a human "wizard" in another room. In the *AI* interviews, Ellie "acts in a fully autonomous way using different automated perception and behaviour generation modules" — no human in the loop. Critically for the challenge design: **training and development sets mix WoZ + AI sessions, but the test set is composed solely of fully autonomous AI sessions.** This makes the test partition a deliberate distribution-shift probe: can a model trained partly on human-driven interviews still score depression when the interviewer is a pure machine?

**Partitions (Table 2).** 275 subjects total / 73:10:33 duration: Training 163 subjects (43:30:20), Development 56 (14:47:31), Test 56 (14:52:42). Partitioned to preserve age, gender, and PHQ-8 distribution. ✔ corroborated (163/56/56, 275 total).

**Target.** PHQ-8 score, range [0, 24]; clinical cutpoints at [5, 10, 15, 20] for mild / moderate / moderately-severe / severe depression. ✔ corroborated.

## 5. The CCC metric (and why it was chosen)

All three sub-challenges are ranked by the **Concordance Correlation Coefficient (CCC)** (Lin 1989), with RMSE additionally reported for DDS. The organizers justify CCC over Spearman's CC or r² because: it is "not biased by changes in scale and location," "elegantly includes information on both precision and accuracy in a single statistical measure," is well rooted in the literature, and "can be easily exploited as a loss function for training neural networks."

CCC combines Pearson correlation with a penalty for any shift in mean or variance between prediction and target:

```
        2 · ρ · σ_x · σ_y
CCC = ─────────────────────────────
       σ_x² + σ_y² + (μ_x − μ_y)²
```

where ρ is Pearson's correlation, σ the standard deviations, μ the means. CCC = 1 only when predictions lie exactly on the 45° line (perfect agreement, not merely perfect rank). The DDS baseline literally **trains with a CCC loss** (the negative/opposite of CCC) and reports RMSE on the original PHQ scale.

## 6. The audio feature sets (the transferable engineering)

For audio (the Pebble-relevant channel), AVEC 2019 provides four standardized representations, all extractable with open-source tooling and reproducible scripts (public GitHub repo `AudioVisualEmotionChallenge/AVEC2019`):

1. **eGeMAPS** (extended Geneva Minimalistic Acoustic Parameter Set; Eyben et al. 2016) — **88 expert-designed acoustic measures** covering spectral, cepstral, prosodic, and voice-quality dimensions. Computed per analysis window via openSMILE. This is the canonical low-dimensional, interpretable, clinically-motivated speech feature set for affect/health.
2. **MFCCs 1–13** plus their 1st- and 2nd-order derivatives (deltas + double-deltas) — classic cepstral low-level descriptors, openSMILE. Summarized over a 4 s sliding window with 1 s hop (E-DAIC).
3. **DeepSpectrum (DS)** — deep representation learning for audio: mel-spectrogram images (128 mel bands, 4 s window, 1 s hop) are fed through pre-trained image CNNs and the activations become the feature vector. AVEC 2019 used **four pre-trained CNNs: VGG-16, AlexNet, DenseNet-121, DenseNet-201**. Dimensions: 4096 from VGG-16 / AlexNet (2nd FC layer), 1024 from DenseNet-121 and 1920 from DenseNet-201 (last avg-pool layer). Toolkit: DeepSpectrum.
4. **Bags-of-Audio-Words (BoAW / XBoW)** — a text-processing technique ported to audio: LLDs (MFCCs or eGeMAPS, standardized online) are vector-quantized against a learned **codebook of size 100**; log term-frequencies are taken to compress range. Computed over 4 s blocks. Toolkit: openXBOW. Two variants: **BoAW-M** (MFCC front-end) and **BoAW-e** (eGeMAPS front-end).

(Video features — 17 FAUs via OpenFace, BoVW, and ResNet/VGG deep visual reps pre-trained on AffWild — exist but are not directly relevant to Pebble's voice modality.)

## 7. The DDS baseline system and results (the headline numbers)

**Baseline architecture (DDS).** A **single-layer 64-dimensional GRU** recurrent network, dropout 20%, followed by a 64-d fully-connected layer producing a single regression value. PHQ-8 labels are **downscaled by a factor of 25** to floating point before training (a normalization trick to "handle bias"). Trained and evaluated with a **CCC loss** (RMSE reported on the original PHQ scale). Batch size 15; learning rate tuned per feature set. Maximum sequence length 20 minutes per session (frames dropped 1-of-2 or 1-of-4 for high-dim deep reps to fit GPU memory). **Fusion = simple averaging of the per-representation regression scores.**

**Results (Table 6, CCC; RMSE in parentheses):**

| MFCCs (audio) | .198 | — | 7.28 | — |
| eGeMAPS (audio) | .076 | — | 7.78 | — |
| BoAW-M (audio) | .102 | — | 6.32 | — |
| BoAW-e (audio) | .272 | .045 | 6.43 | 8.19 |
| DS-DNet (DenseNet-121) | .165 | — | 8.09 | — |
| **DS-VGG (audio, best audio dev)** | **.305** | .108 | 8.00 | 9.33 |

Status of load-bearing numbers: ✔ corroborated — Fusion **test RMSE = 6.37**, dev RMSE = 5.03, dev fusion CCC = .336; AVEC 2017 baseline RMSE = 6.97; AVEC 2017 best DSC RMSE = 4.99 (all confirmed against arXiv:1907.11510 §5.2 and the Semantic Scholar / sites.google.com/view/avec2019 challenge pages).

**Key qualitative findings the authors draw:**
- On the **development** set, best audio CCC is **DS-VGG (.305)** and best video CCC is **ResNet (.269)**; fusion is best of all (.336). This shows "the power of representations learnt by deep neural networks with a large amount of data when being used in a different context to which they were initially designed."
- On the **test** set (autonomous-AI-only), the **ResNet video model wins (CCC .120)** "despite a relatively low CCC" — i.e., everything degrades sharply on the held-out autonomous-agent partition.
- Fusion test **RMSE 6.37** is slightly better than the AVEC 2017 baseline on DAIC-WoZ (RMSE 6.97), **but** the 2019 baseline is a more complex GRU-RNN vs. 2017's linear regression, and the relevant ceiling is the AVEC 2017 best entry, **RMSE 4.99**.
- **The central conclusion:** "recognition seems more challenging when the agent is solely AI driven, than when a human is driving the agent as a WoZ." The authors hypothesize a human wizard "might drive the virtual agent to a situation that eases the observation of patterns associated with depression," whereas the autonomous agent may conduct the interview less optimally — and they raise designing the agent (e.g. via reinforcement learning) to **maximize the observability of depression cues**.

## 8. Parts directly useful for Pebble (tagged with Decision IDs)

> Pebble v1 trains learned `emotion` (12-label) + `severity` (regression) heads on **text**; the voice-message modality is a thesis-level analogue, and this paper is the reference benchmark for it.

1. **CCC as the severity metric and loss (D-D).** AVEC 2019 ranks depression-severity regression by CCC and trains with a CCC loss, explicitly preferring it to Pearson alone because it penalizes scale/location shift. Pebble's severity head currently targets **Pearson** (per D-D). This paper is the published precedent that for a clinical-severity regression where calibration of the absolute value matters (not just rank), **CCC is the more honest metric and a usable differentiable loss**. *Transfer risk: moderate.* CCC is computed batch-wise and is noisier than MSE on small batches; E-DAIC test CCC is tiny (.12), so CCC is a sensitive, high-variance metric on small severity sets — exactly Pebble's C-SSRS regime (~100 rows). Report both Pearson and CCC; consider CCC as an auxiliary/eval metric rather than the sole training loss.

2. **eGeMAPS as the interpretable audio baseline (D-A / D-H — voice extension).** 88 expert acoustic parameters, openSMILE, reproducible. *Transfer risk: high relevance, but Pebble v1 is text-only.* If/when Pebble adds a voice-message head, eGeMAPS is the cheap, clinically-grounded, low-dimensional starting point before any deep audio model — and it is the feature set whose prosodic markers (reduced pitch, reduced pitch range, slower speaking rate; §2.2) are the literature-validated depressed-speech correlates. This is a D-H "datasets/anchors" item for a future voice modality, not a v1 head.

3. **PHQ-8 → [0,24] target with [5,10,15,20] cutpoints + label downscaling by 25 (D-C / D-D).** The DDS converts an ordinal-bucketed clinical instrument into a bounded continuous regression target and normalizes it for training. *Transfer risk: moderate.* Pebble's severity is a regression with SemEval/WASSA intensity transfer; the AVEC pattern (continuous regression over a clinically-bucketed scale, with documented cutpoints so you can recover ordinal buckets post-hoc) is directly reusable for mapping C-SSRS-like severity to a continuous head while preserving ordinal thresholds (the D-C bars: 52% acc / 0.75 wF1 / 47.8% macro-recall imply you also need bucketed eval — keep the cutpoints).

4. **The WoZ→autonomous-AI degradation finding (Pebble thesis-level, voice modality).** Moving the interviewer from human-driven to fully autonomous **lowers** depression-detection accuracy (test ResNet CCC .12; everything else near-zero). *Transfer risk: directly applicable and cautionary.* Pebble's voice-message scenario is the **autonomous** end of this spectrum — there is no human interviewer eliciting cues, and the child speaks freely. AVEC 2019 is the published warning that ecological, autonomous-agent speech is materially harder than clinician-elicited speech. This bounds expectations for any Pebble voice head and argues for (a) eliciting structure where possible and (b) not over-promising single-utterance severity accuracy.

5. **Fusion = score-averaging across representations (D-B-adjacent).** The DDS baseline fuses by averaging per-feature regression scores; the fusion beat every single representation on dev (CCC .336 vs best single .305). *Transfer risk: low-cost.* For a future Pebble voice head, late-fusion averaging of an eGeMAPS-regressor and a deep-spectrum-regressor is a trivially cheap ensemble that the benchmark shows helps. (Not a v1 MTL-balancing item, but a concrete fusion recipe.)

## 9. How each part helps Pebble succeed (concrete actions)

- **Severity head (D-D): add CCC alongside Pearson.** In `severity`'s eval, log CCC next to Pearson; if the head's predictions are well-ranked but mis-scaled (high Pearson, low CCC), that's a calibration gap the Decision Engine cares about. Optionally add a CCC term to the severity loss as the AVEC baseline does. Concrete artifact: extend the severity-head metric block to emit `{pearson, ccc, rmse}`.
- **Severity head (D-C): keep clinical cutpoints with the continuous target.** Mirror AVEC's [5,10,15,20]-style bucketing so the regression output can be thresholded back into ordinal severity bands for the D-C accuracy/wF1/macro-recall bars. Artifact: a `severity_buckets` config + a bucketed-eval pass.
- **Future voice-message head: start with eGeMAPS + openSMILE, score-average with DeepSpectrum.** Before training any heavy audio encoder, build an eGeMAPS-88 → small regressor baseline and a DeepSpectrum baseline, fuse by averaging (AVEC recipe). This gives an interpretable, reproducible floor and the prosodic features clinicians can read. Artifact: a `voice/` experiment stub using openSMILE + DeepSpectrum, CCC-evaluated.
- **Thesis framing: cite the autonomous-agent penalty.** When justifying the voice-message modality's difficulty, cite AVEC 2019's WoZ→AI degradation as the published evidence that autonomous-agent speech depression detection is harder than clinician-elicited — and design the companion app to *elicit* (gentle structured prompts) rather than rely purely on free monologue.

## 10. Child mental-health lens (transfer validity, risks, mitigations, ethics)

- **Population mismatch (the dominant risk).** E-DAIC is **US military veterans / adults** interviewed about distress, anxiety, PTSD, depression. Pebble's voice-message users are **children**. Child speech differs in fundamental frequency (much higher pitch), articulation, vocabulary, prosodic range, and emotional expression — eGeMAPS pitch-based depressed-speech markers (reduced pitch, reduced range, slower rate) were calibrated on adults and **may not transfer**. The depressed-speech prosodic literature AVEC cites (Cummins et al. 2015) is adult. *Mitigation:* any Pebble voice head must be re-validated / re-calibrated on child speech; treat AVEC absolute numbers as adult upper-bound analogues, not bars.
- **Autonomous = ecologically valid for Pebble, and harder.** AVEC 2019's most Pebble-relevant single fact: the autonomous-AI test partition is where accuracy collapses. Pebble's voice messages are even **less structured** than an Ellie interview (no agent eliciting cues at all). This is the ecologically-valid but lowest-signal regime; expectations for single-message severity must stay modest.
- **Tiny, fragile severity signal.** Test CCC ≈ .12 means even the field's standardized benchmark barely beats chance on autonomous audio. For a child-facing safety product, a learned voice-severity head at this signal level cannot be a sole decision input — it must feed a Decision Engine with conservative thresholds and human/caregiver escalation, never a standalone verdict. (Consistent with Pebble's v1 stance: no learned safety head; safety is heuristic + escalation.)
- **No turn-level scoring.** AVEC scores **whole sessions** (up to 20 min), not turns/messages. Pebble scores **turn-level / mid-conversation**. A session-level CCC of .12 does not imply per-message severity is recoverable; segment-level depression cues are sparser still.
- **Ethics.** E-DAIC is a gated, consented research corpus (US Army-supported). Pebble cannot reuse it for a child product. Recording children's voice messages adds biometric-data sensitivity beyond text: voiceprints are identifying. Any voice modality needs explicit guardian consent, on-device or strongly-controlled processing, and minimization — a heavier bar than Pebble's text path.

## 11. Limitations & open questions for Pebble (incl. contradictions/gaps)

- **Contradiction vs Pebble's metric plan (D-D).** Pebble's D-D specifies **Pearson** as the severity metric; AVEC 2019 deliberately **rejects** rank-only metrics in favor of CCC because Pearson "is biased by changes in scale and location" and hides mis-calibration. This is a direct gap: if Pebble reports only Pearson, it can claim a well-ranked severity head that is systematically mis-scaled — exactly the failure CCC was designed to catch. Pebble should reconcile by adding CCC, at minimum as a reported eval metric.
- **Contradiction vs FAIIR (paper 01) on what's achievable.** FAIIR shows **child-register** crisis signal is recoverable at high AUROC (Suicide 0.94, Self-Harm 0.97) — but on **text**. AVEC 2019 shows **adult-speech** depression severity is barely recoverable (test CCC .12) on **autonomous audio**. The gap Pebble must respect: text classification of explicit crisis content is far easier than continuous severity regression from free speech. Pebble's voice modality should not inherit text-modality optimism.
- **WoZ vs AI confound is uncontrolled.** Training/dev mix WoZ + AI sessions; test is AI-only. The reported degradation conflates (a) genuine autonomous-agent difficulty with (b) train/test distribution shift. The paper does not isolate the two. For Pebble this means the "autonomous is harder" claim is suggestive, not cleanly quantified.
- **eGeMAPS underperforms here.** Notably, in DDS the expert eGeMAPS feature set scores **worst on dev** (CCC .076), beaten by MFCCs (.198), BoAW-e (.272), and DS-VGG (.305). So the clinically-interpretable set is not the best predictor on this task — a caution that interpretability ≠ accuracy, and Pebble shouldn't assume eGeMAPS alone suffices for a voice head.
- **No calibration / reliability analysis.** Like FAIIR, AVEC reports point metrics (CCC/RMSE) but no reliability diagrams or ECE — yet Pebble's Decision Engine consumes calibrated severity. This remains Pebble's to add.
- **Open question worth pursuing:** is E-DAIC's transcript channel (Google Cloud ASR) usable? AVEC's baselines are audio/video only, but E-DAIC ships auto-transcripts — a text-from-voice path that bridges Pebble's text heads to the voice modality without a dedicated audio encoder. The paper doesn't explore it; Pebble could.
