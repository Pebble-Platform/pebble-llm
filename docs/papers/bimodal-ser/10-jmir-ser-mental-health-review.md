### What the paper actually does

A PRISMA systematic review of **audio-only** SER applied to psychiatric diagnosis/prognosis. Scope and pipeline (Methods; Results §PRISMA):
- **Search:** PubMed + IEEE Xplore + arXiv + ScienceDirect to **Feb 2025**; query `("emotion recognition" OR "affective computing" OR "emotional analysis") AND ("psychiatry" OR "psychology") AND ("speech" OR "voice")`. **3648 screened → 85 retrieved → 14 included** (Results, Fig. 3). ✔ (PDF p.5).
- **Hard exclusions that matter for us (Textbox 1):** *"Audio was only analyzed in conjunction with another modality (eg, text)"* is **excluded**, and reviews-without-experiment and neurological disorders (e.g. Alzheimer) are excluded. So this is a review of **unimodal-acoustic clinical** SER — by construction it says nothing about bimodal audio+text systems like ViEmoSpeech. ✔ (PDF p.5).
- **Condition split (Abstract; Results):** suicide risk/SI **3/14**, depression & mood **8/14**, psychotic disorders **3/14**. ✔ (the review prints both "18%/53%/18%" and "21%/57%/21%" roundings in different places — an internal rounding inconsistency, not a data conflict).
- **Risk of bias (QUADAS-2, Fig. 4):** most studies low-risk overall; the one systematic failure is **patient selection — 5/14 high risk** (3 had **no control group**, 2 non-representative samples), plus 2 high applicability concerns and 4 unclear. ✔ (PDF p.6). Full table in Appendix 1 (not opened).
- **Direct vs indirect SER (Operational Definitions):** "direct" = emotion is an explicit modelled task; "indirect" = emotion-bearing acoustic features (openSMILE/eGeMAPS) fed straight to a pathology classifier with **no emotion label anywhere**. Most reviewed studies are *indirect*; only Gideon et al. is genuinely speech→emotion→pathology. ✔

**Headline per-condition results (numbers as re-reported by the review; status tagged):**
- *Suicide/SI (3):* Gerczuk — **81% balanced acc** high-vs-low risk, gender-exclusive wav2vec2.0 ✔; Gideon [20] — **AUC 0.78** classifying emotion labels, **AUC 0.79** using *emotional variability* to separate SI vs non-SI, SI group shows **lower emotional variability / flatter affect** ≈ (review-only, primary not re-pulled); Belouali [3] — **sens 0.86 / spec 0.70 / AUC 0.80** ✔, SI voices "flatter, less animated, more monotonous" (lower SD/kurtosis/skewness of voiced-segment energy). Envelope: **AUC ≈ 0.8, acc ≈ 80%**.
- *Depression (8):* Wang [6] transformer **acc 77% / F1 0.63** (PHQ-8) ≈; Yang [46] LSTM+biLSTM **77%** 3-way bipolar/unipolar/HC ≈; Yang [47] switching-pause + F0, pause explains **32%** of within-subject HRSD variance, LDA **69.5%** severity ≈; Mao [49] hybrid DL on DAIC-WOZ **acc 98.7% / F1 0.987** ≈ **(near-perfect → treat as a leakage/over-fit red flag, not an achievable bar)**; Yang [50] transformer **F1 0.78** (DAIC-WOZ) / **0.87** (proprietary), proposes the **600–700 Hz** band (Mandarin vowel /e/,/ɤ/) as a depression biomarker ≈; Zhou [21] F2 & spectral-flux negatively, MFCC-4 positively associated with depression ≈. Trend "69% (2013, 2 prosodic feats) → 98% (2023, DL)".
- *Psychosis (3):* Chakraborty [51] linear-SVM+PCA on emobase **79.49%** (vs 66.67% majority-class), NSA-16 severity **62–85%** ≈; de Boer [53] eGeMAPS+RF **86%** HC-vs-patient, **74%** neg-vs-pos symptoms ≈; Çokal [18] pause-pattern statistics only (no ML metric), **15/group, 60 total**. **None of the 3 psychosis studies did direct emotion analysis.** ✔

**Datasets catalogued (narrative review):** DAIC-WOZ (193 interviews, 5–20 min, NA English, depression/anxiety/PTSD), RAVDESS (24 actors, acted), **FAU-AIBO (spontaneous German *children* speaking to a robot, 11 emotional states, 5 judges, word-level — the 2009 INTERSPEECH challenge, "even SOTA struggles")**, IEMOCAP (~12 h, scripted+improvised, **both categorical and dimensional labels**), CaFE (Canadian French, 6M+6F, 6 Ekman + neutral, 69 min). The review states outright *"few to no datasets serve both SER and mental health applications."* ✔

**Tonal/non-English coverage (the point of most interest to us):** the review explicitly notes *"the predominance of English and Mandarin datasets"* (Results §PRISMA Selected Studies) and, in Future Directions, warns generalization across languages/cultures is *"a significant hurdle… composed of participants from a single cultural context."* The **only** engagement with a tonal language is Yang [50]'s Mandarin-vowel-formant depression biomarker — a *within-Mandarin* acoustic finding, **not** a tone×emotion method. **No Vietnamese study, no tone-as-lexical-channel treatment anywhere in the 14.** ✔ This is a clean whitespace confirmation for ViEmoSpeech's V-D novelty claim.

**Recommendations checklist (Discussion / Future Directions):** (1) run **shared challenges** to fix non-comparability (they cite AVEC/ADReSS as models); (2) prefer the **speech→emotion→pathology** pipeline over speech→pathology because it is *interpretable* and clinician-collaborative — but warns it **compounds SER error**, so "selection of the SER system is of utmost importance"; (3) adopt **dimensional** frameworks (RDoC, HiTOP) alongside categorical DSM labels, while conceding "no clear systematic mapping from emotions to dimensional pathology" and that the circumplex "does not directly align with DSM/RDoC"; (4) manage the **interpretability↔performance trade-off** (openSMILE/eGeMAPS interpretable biomarkers vs opaque DL — note Mao's 98.7% is the opaque end); (5) validate across **cultures/languages**; (6) privacy/ethics/data-sharing for confidential clinical audio.

### Parts directly useful for ViEmoSpeech (tagged with Decision IDs)

1. **The performance envelope for clinical-adjacent distress detection — as a ceiling we must NOT claim, and a target we may *reference*.** Suicide/SI band **AUC ≈ 0.80, acc ≈ 80%**, best specific points **Belouali sens 0.86 / AUC 0.80 ✔** and **Gerczuk 81% bal-acc ✔**. **[V-F, V-G]** These are the published bars *for the clinical task*; ViEmoSpeech's distress head is an **acted-drama proxy**, so these are an *analogy anchor* (what real distress-from-voice discrimination looks like), never a comparator our number is placed beside. Transfer risk: **high if borrowed as a target** (different population, different label semantics); **low if used only to frame "screening favours sensitivity"** — which is exactly what the recall-floor objective encodes.
2. **"Flat/monotonous affect" is the reproducible acoustic signature of distress across the SI studies.** Belouali (lower energy SD/kurtosis/skewness in voiced segments) + Gideon (SI group has **lower emotional variability**) both converge on *reduced prosodic range* as the distress marker. **[V-F, V-B]** This tells us which acoustic dimension our distress head should be sensitive to, and argues the head should see **prosodic-variability features (F0/energy SD across the utterance)**, not just a pooled emotion embedding. Transfer risk: **moderate** — the finding was measured on *spontaneous clinical* speech; in *acted drama* the actor may over-produce range, so "flat affect ⇒ distress" may not hold and must be verified on our gold set, not assumed.
3. **The categorical↔dimensional label debate, resolved in favour of "carry both."** IEMOCAP is cited as the model dataset precisely because it carries **both categorical and dimensional labels** (Fig. 1 Plutchik wheel, Fig. 2 Russell circumplex); RDoC/HiTOP argue clinical severity is a *continuum*. **[V-E]** External support for ViEmoSpeech's chosen scheme (**7-class + V/A 1–5 + distress**) over a categorical-only or dimensional-only design — with the review's own caveat that the two spaces **do not map cleanly onto each other**. Transfer risk: **low** — this is conceptual label-design support that holds regardless of language/register; the caveat (no clean categorical→dimensional map) directly warns us **not** to derive distress from V/A.
4. **The speech→emotion→pathology *interpretable* pipeline, and its stated failure mode.** The review's preferred design routes emotion as an explicit intermediate so a clinician can read *why*; its named risk is **error compounding** from the SER stage. **[V-G, V-A]** For us this maps onto the ASR→text branch: our pipeline already has a compounding-error stage (PhoWhisper tone-swap errors at high arousal), so the review's warning is the eval-protocol justification for reporting distress/emotion performance **conditioned on ASR-error slices**. Transfer risk: **low** — the "compounding error through a cascade" principle is architecture-agnostic and applies directly to our ASR→text branch.
5. **The QUADAS-2 patient-selection failure is exactly the pitfall our eval protocol claims to avoid.** **5/14 high-risk on patient selection, 3 with no control group, small N (Gerczuk N=20, Çokal N=60).** **[V-G]** ViEmoSpeech's speaker-disjoint + whole-series-holdout design and its ≥50-clip rare-class floor are the concrete answers to "small-N, no external validation, non-representative sampling." This review is the clinician-authored, QUADAS-2-backed citation that names those failure modes. Transfer risk: **low** — methodological-hygiene lessons transfer across domains unchanged.
6. **The tonal/non-English whitespace.** Predominance of English+Mandarin, single-culture datasets, **zero tone-as-lexical-channel method, zero Vietnamese.** **[V-H, V-D]** Direct external evidence for ViEmoSpeech's corpus-existence and method-novelty claims. Transfer risk: **low** — it is an absence-of-evidence claim about the field, which is precisely what a systematic review is authoritative for.

### How each part helps ViEmoSpeech succeed

- **Distress head framing (1,2) → V-F.** Write the distress-head spec with the Belouali/Gerczuk band cited as *"what voice-based distress discrimination achieves in the clinical literature (AUC≈0.80; sens 0.86)"* and immediately state the boundary: our distress flag is an **acted-drama proxy under a recall-floor objective**, evaluated by **recall@floor on held-out gold speakers**, *not* by clinical sensitivity/specificity against a diagnosis. Concretely, add to the distress-head input an **utterance-level prosodic-variability vector** (F0 SD, voiced-energy SD/kurtosis/skewness) — the review's most reproducible distress signature — and test whether it lifts distress recall over the emotion-embedding-only baseline. Runnable ablation, not a citation.
- **Label scheme (3) → V-E.** Keep the **7-class + V/A + distress** scheme; cite IEMOCAP's dual labelling + RDoC/HiTOP as the external precedent that mature affect resources carry both spaces. But **do not** claim our V/A axis maps onto any clinical severity construct — the review explicitly denies that mapping exists; our distress flag stays a separate, self-defined proxy label, not a derived function of V/A.
- **Error-compounding + eval slicing (4,5) → V-G.** Bake into the eval protocol: (a) report distress/emotion metrics **split by ASR-error presence** (gold-caption vs PhoWhisper transcript input) so the compounding-error stage the review warns about is measured, not hidden; (b) keep the speaker-disjoint + whole-series holdout as the answer to QUADAS-2 patient-selection bias, and state the min-N per class explicitly (the review's N=20 Gerczuk study is the cautionary anchor).
- **Non-comparability → V-G, V-H.** The review's central complaint is that heterogeneous datasets/metrics make SER-in-mental-health results non-comparable. ViEmoSpeech answers this by anchoring to **shared, comparable axes** (MSP-Podcast V/A/D for cross-corpus V/A; macro-F1 + CCC + recall@floor reported uniformly). Cite this review as the motivation for that comparability discipline.
- **Whitespace → V-H, V-D.** Use "predominance of English+Mandarin, no tonal-lexical method, no Vietnamese" as a one-line positioning sentence in the corpus/method paper intro.

### Child mental-health lens (transfer validity, risks, mitigations, ethics)

- **Register mismatch is total and must be stated up front.** Every reviewed dataset is **clinical or acted-adult** — DAIC-WOZ clinical interviews, RAVDESS/CaFE professional actors, veteran/schizophrenia/depression patient cohorts. The single child dataset (FAU-AIBO, German children with a robot) is **spontaneous child speech**, and the review notes even SOTA struggles on it. ViEmoSpeech is **acted VN TV-drama speech** — neither clinical nor spontaneous-child. So **none of the review's diagnostic performance numbers transfer** to ViEmoSpeech's regime; they are context, not comparators.
- **The clinical claims we must explicitly NOT borrow (this is the core transfer-risk finding):** (i) *any* statement that a voice-emotion model "detects depression/suicide risk/psychosis" — our distress flag is a drama-acted category with no clinical anchor, no PHQ-8/C-SSRS/HRSD ground truth, no patient cohort; (ii) the "speech biomarker" language (spectral-slope/formant/pause biomarkers) — those were derived on diagnosed patients, ours would be derived on actors performing distress; (iii) the AUC/sensitivity envelope as *our* target. This is precisely the anti-pattern already flagged for vn-10 Dynamic-CBAM ("5 acted emotions sold as depression diagnosis"); this review is the higher-authority (JMIR, clinician-authored, QUADAS-2) citation for **why acted-emotion ≠ clinical diagnosis** and for how honest clinical-adjacent SER is *supposed* to be validated (patient vs control, external cohort, risk-of-bias assessment).
- **Mitigation / honest framing:** ViEmoSpeech's distress flag should be documented as *"an acted, dramatized distress category; a recall-floored screening-style flag for downstream human review, NOT a clinical or diagnostic label,"* with this review cited as the standard the label deliberately does **not** meet. The recall-floor objective is the right shape (screening favours sensitivity — the review's own studies report sensitivity 0.86 over precision), but the *label semantics* stay proxy.
- **Ethics:** the review's confidentiality/data-sharing concern is about clinical patient audio; ViEmoSpeech sidesteps it by construction (public TV-drama, release = features+timestamps+labels+speaker-ids, CC-BY, no media). The residual ethical risk is **overclaim** — presenting a drama-distress model as clinically meaningful. The mitigation is documentary discipline, not data handling.

### Limitations & open questions for ViEmoSpeech

- **Contradiction / gap vs ViEmoSpeech's bimodal thesis (the required one):** this review **excludes multimodal audio+text studies by design** (Textbox 1: "Audio was only analyzed in conjunction with another modality (eg, text)" → excluded), yet in Future Directions concedes *"a multimodal approach could outperform approaches based solely on the speech signal (eg, Mao et al)"* and points to CLAP-style audio+text pretraining. So the review's entire evidence base is **unimodal-acoustic**, and it cannot adjudicate ViEmoSpeech's central bet — that in a **tonal** language the **text/semantic branch must carry more load** because tone and emotion share the phonation channel (vn-06 Shen, V-D). The review provides **no evidence for or against** the tone×emotion claim; its silence is the gap ViEmoSpeech occupies, but it also means we cannot cite this review *in support of* fusion — only to establish the acoustic-only baseline it reviews. This also sits opposite vn-12's "semantics dominate" and vn-08's "text near-useless" — three sources, none of which measured tonal-lexical channel competition, so our measurable V-D claim stays genuinely open.
- **The 98.7% depression result (Mao [49]) is almost certainly leakage/over-fit** (DAIC-WOZ has 193 interviews; near-perfect accuracy on a 5–20-min clinical-interview corpus is implausible without speaker leakage) — the review reports it uncritically as "impressive." This is the same speaker-leakage pattern ViEmoSpeech's speaker-disjoint split exists to prevent; **do not** cite Mao's number as an attainable target. [V-G]
- **Small-N is endemic and the review under-weights it.** Gerczuk's headline 81% is **N=20** (from the primary source, omitted by the review); Çokal is N=60. Our rare-class ≥50-clip floor and whole-series holdout are the corrective, but it means the "envelope" numbers are themselves fragile. [V-G]
- **Appendix 2 (Table S1, per-study methods/datasets/languages overview) was not opened** — if a precise per-study language/dataset/model breakdown is ever needed for the related-work table, that supplementary PDF is the source. Low-priority; the narrative already gives the language distribution (English + Mandarin dominant).
- **No turn-level or child-register evidence anywhere.** All 14 studies are speaker-level or interview-level clinical classification; none score emotion mid-utterance and none use child speakers in the target regime. ViEmoSpeech's turn-level, child-adjacent (drama) scoring has no precedent here to lean on — neither a bar nor a method transfers.
