# Paper 39 — Self-Supervised Representations in Speech-Based Depression Detection

## 1. Bibliographic info

**Title:** Self-Supervised Representations in Speech-Based Depression Detection

**Authors:** Wen Wu, Chao Zhang, Philip C. Woodland — all of the Department of Engineering, University of Cambridge (Trumpington St., Cambridge, UK). Contact: `{ww368,cz277,pcw}@eng.cam.ac.uk`.

**Year / venue:** ICASSP 2023 (IEEE International Conference on Acoustics, Speech and Signal Processing), June 04–10, 2023, Rhodes, Greece. DOI 10.1109/ICASSP49357.2023.10094910. Preprint arXiv:2305.12263 (v2, 6 Jul 2023; cs.CL). Local PDF is the arXiv v2.

**Index terms (verbatim):** "Speech-based depression detection, self-supervised learning, foundation model."

**One-line summary:** Replaces handcrafted acoustic features for speech-based depression detection (SDD) with intermediate-layer SSL representations from wav2vec 2.0 / HuBERT / WavLM, does a block-wise (per-layer) analysis to find which layers carry the depression signal, transfers knowledge from ASR and emotion recognition by fine-tuning the backbones, augments the tiny DAIC-WOZ set with sub-dialogue shuffling, and ensembles speech + text foundation models to reach a SOTA F1 of **0.89** on DAIC-WOZ **using ASR (not oracle) transcriptions**.

## 2. Why this paper is in the Pebble set

Pebble's thesis includes a **voice-message modality**: a child can send a short spoken audio clip instead of typing. For the *depression / severity* signal on that modality, Pebble must decide how to turn raw audio into a feature vector that a head can score. The classical answer is handcrafted descriptors (COVAREP, eGeMAPS, glottal/prosodic features); the modern answer — and this paper's whole point — is to drop those and use **intermediate-layer embeddings from a self-supervised speech foundation model** (wav2vec2 / HuBERT / WavLM). This paper is the cleanest published recipe for exactly that swap on a *clinical depression* target, and it answers the three questions Pebble actually has to answer to build a voice depression head:

1. **Which backbone, and which layer?** (the audio analog of Pebble's NeoBERT/ModernBERT/MentalBERT text-encoder bake-off — **D-A**).
2. **How do you survive extreme data scarcity + class imbalance?** (DAIC-WOZ = 189 samples, 28% positive — far worse than Pebble's silver corpus, so its augmentation + transfer recipe is a worked example of the regime Pebble's severity head lives in — **D-B**, **D-D**).
3. **What do you transfer *from*, given no in-domain depression data at scale?** (ASR and emotion-recognition fine-tuning as the transfer sources — directly parallel to Pebble's severity head transferring from SemEval/WASSA intensity — **D-D**, **D-H**).

This is adult clinical-interview English (Wizard-of-Oz virtual interviewer), **not** child speech, **not** turn-level mid-conversation, and labelled at the **session level** (one label per 50-minute interview). Its value to Pebble is the *method and the relative ranking of design choices*, not the absolute DAIC-WOZ numbers, and the transfer caveats below are load-bearing.

### What the paper actually does

**Task framing (§2.1).** SDD is a **binary** classification — depressed vs non-depressed — at the **dialogue (session) level**. Input is a whole clinical interview `X = {x1,...,xT}`, a sequence of `T` utterances. Each utterance `xt` is fed to a frozen (or fine-tuned-then-frozen) **foundation model**, producing a `(t, D)` frame-level tensor; **temporal average pooling** collapses it to a single `D`-dim vector per utterance. The `T`-length sequence of `D`-dim vectors then goes to a small **depression-detection block**: two 128-dim Transformer encoder blocks, 4 attention heads each, then a fully-connected output layer (**0.3M params total**). The depression block is the only thing trained for SDD; the foundation model is frozen during SDD training.

**Foundation models (§2.1).** Three SSL speech backbones, all **base** versions: **12 Transformer encoder blocks, 768-dim, ~95M params** each.
- **wav2vec 2.0** (W2V2) — `facebook/wav2vec2-base`.
- **HuBERT** — `facebook/hubert-base-ls960`.
- **WavLM** — `microsoft/wavlm-base-plus`.
Fine-tuned variants used as transfer sources: **W2V2ASR** (`facebook/wav2vec2-base-960h`, fine-tuned for ASR on 960h LibriSpeech), **W2V2AER** (W2V2 base fine-tuned for emotion on 110h MSP-Podcast with 2 extra FC layers), **WavLMAER** (WavLM base fine-tuned the same way). The **text** foundation model is **RoBERTa base** (`roberta-base`).

**Dataset (§2.2).** **DAIC-WOZ** [16]: 189 clinical interviews (virtual-human "SimSensei" interviewer). Train set 107 interviews, **30 depressed**; dev set 35 interviews, **12 depressed**. Evaluation is **F1 on the development subset** (per prior work [7,17–20]). Each model is trained for **20 random seeds**; the paper reports **F1-max** (best seed), **F1-avg** (mean over seeds), and **F1-std** (seed-to-seed std). Reporting F1-std across 20 seeds is a notable methodological strength on such a small set.

**Data scarcity + imbalance (§2.3).** Stated plainly: "DAIC-WOZ consists of 50+ hours of speech recordings that correspond to merely **189 samples**," and positives are 28% vs 72% negatives in training. The fix is **sub-dialogue shuffling** (Algorithm 1): from each full dialogue `x1:T`, sample `M` sub-dialogues `xs:e`, where the sub-dialogue length `d` is drawn as a coefficient from `[l, h)` times `T-1` and the start `s` is random. The number of sub-dialogues is **class-balanced**: `M+` per positive dialogue is set, and `M- = N+·M+/N-` per negative dialogue, so the augmented set is balanced regardless of the raw 28/72 skew. This is augmentation-as-rebalancing.

**Block-wise analysis (§3, Table 1, Fig. 2).** The central contribution — the first per-layer analysis for SDD. Each intermediate Transformer block's output is fed (alone) to the SDD block, blocks 2/4/6/8/10/12. Findings (F1-avg, frozen pre-trained models):
- **WavLMPT** is the best pre-trained backbone overall; its peak is at **block 8** (F1-avg **0.700**, F1-max 0.750).
- **W2V2PT** peaks at **block 8** (F1-avg 0.627, F1-max 0.667).
- **HuBERTPT** peaks later, at **block 10** (F1-avg 0.667, F1-max 0.762).
- For all three, F1 rises with layer depth then falls — a clear inverted-U. The interpretation (citing Pasad et al. [21]): shallow layers encode acoustic/FBank-like info, **middle layers (~block 8) encode word-meaning information**, deepest layers drift to word/phonetic identities. The conclusion: **layers carrying word-meaning information are the most useful for SDD**, not the shallowest acoustic or the deepest layers.

**Effect of augmentation amount (§3.1, Table 2; WavLM block 12).** F1-avg by `M+`: 100→0.451, 200→0.583, 500→0.647, **1000→0.679 (peak; F1-max 0.762)**, 1500→0.669; F1-std falls from 0.131 (M+=100) to 0.027 (M+=1000). More augmentation sharply improves *and stabilizes* performance up to 1000, then over-replication risks overfitting. They use **M+=500** in subsequent experiments (performance/training-time trade).

**ASR / AER fine-tuning (§3.3, Table 1 bottom).** Fine-tuning the backbone for ASR shifts its useful SDD signal to **earlier** blocks (W2V2ASR peaks block 6, F1-avg 0.639); fine-tuning for **emotion (AER)** shifts it **later** and *raises the ceiling* (WavLMAER block 10 F1-avg **0.720**, F1-max 0.769 — the best single-model audio result). Key claim: "Fine-tuning the foundation model for AER improves the overall performance, indicating that **emotion and depression share some para-linguistic indicators**." The AER models report concordance-correlation on MSP-Podcast valence/activation/dominance of 0.418/0.658/0.562 (W2V2AER) and 0.445/0.667/0.597 (WavLMAER).

**ASR transcriptions (§4, Table 3).** Text is known to help SDD, but reference transcripts aren't available in practice. They run their own W2V2ASR to transcribe DAIC-WOZ — WER **3.4%** on LibriSpeech test-clean, 8.6% test-other, but **40.9% on DAIC-WOZ** (huge domain shift). Encoding with RoBERTa: **RoBERTaRef** (oracle text) F1-avg 0.635; **RoBERTaHyp** (ASR text) F1-avg 0.599 — a 0.036 drop and higher variance from using erroneous ASR. **But** concatenating ASR-text RoBERTa with the 6th-block W2V2ASR audio representation, `Cat{RoBERTaHyp, W2V2A6SR}`, reaches **F1-avg 0.648 / F1-max 0.714** — *better than oracle reference text alone*. This is the abstract's central result: ASR ≈ oracle once you add the ASR model's hidden audio representation.

**Speech + text fusion (§5, Table 4).** Concatenating speech-SSL with ASR-text RoBERTa: `Cat{WavLM8PT, RoBERTaHyp}` F1-avg 0.725; **`Cat{WavLMA10ER, RoBERTaHyp}` F1-avg 0.756 / F1-max 0.800** — the best fused single system. Fusion improves F1-avg and F1-max and lowers F1-std (more stable).

**Ensemble (§5, Table 5).** Final SOTA via **voting** ensembles, *no reference transcriptions used*:
- Ensemble 1 (three speech backbones: W2V2 block 6 + HuBERT block 10 + WavLM block 8): **F1-avg 0.800 / F1-max 0.857**.
- Ensemble 2 (three "modalities": WavLM8PT audio + `Cat{WavLMA10ER, RoBERTaHyp}` text + WavLMAER emotion): **F1-avg 0.829 / F1-max 0.886**.

**Cross-comparison (§5, Table 6).** Prior work F1-max: [19] 0.69 (F1-avg), [18] 0.70, [7] 0.77, [17] 0.85, [20] 0.87; **ours F1-avg 0.83 / F1-max 0.89**. The headline: **SOTA F1 0.89 on DAIC-WOZ using only speech input (real ASR, no oracle transcripts)** [✔ corroborated against abstract + ICASSP DOI; table decimals ≈].

### Parts directly useful for Pebble

1. **Block-wise / per-layer selection — use a middle layer (~8), not the last (D-A).** [≈, Table 1 / Fig. 2] The single most transferable methodological result: for a frozen SSL speech backbone, the best SDD signal is in **middle layers (block 8 for WavLM/W2V2, block 10 for HuBERT)**, not block 12. Default "take the last layer" loses ~0.07–0.10 F1. Tag: **D-A** (backbone/representation choice for the voice-message head). Pebble must sweep layers, not assume the final layer, for any frozen-backbone audio feature.

2. **WavLM > HuBERT > W2V2 as the frozen backbone (D-A).** [≈, Table 1] WavLMPT (block 8, F1-avg 0.700) beats W2V2PT (0.627) and HuBERT (0.667). This matches the WavLM denoising-pretraining advantage seen in the SER papers (26/27) and gives Pebble a default audio backbone. Tag: **D-A**.

3. **Class-balancing augmentation by construction (D-B).** [≈, Algorithm 1 + Table 2] Sub-dialogue shuffling sets `M- = N+·M+/N-`, so the *augmented* set is balanced no matter the raw skew, and F1-std collapses from 0.131→0.027 as augmentation rises to M+=1000. This is a concrete imbalance-handling lever that is **data-side** (resampling) rather than **loss-side** (λ weighting / focal). Tag: **D-B** (imbalance handling for the severity/depression head — a data-augmentation alternative/complement to loss weighting).

4. **Transfer from emotion recognition into the depression target (D-D).** [≈, Table 1 bottom] Fine-tuning the backbone for **AER first** raises the SDD ceiling (WavLMAER 0.720 vs WavLMPT 0.700) because "emotion and depression share some para-linguistic indicators." This is the audio mirror of Pebble's plan: the *emotion* head and the *severity* head are not independent — the emotion representation is a useful warm-start / shared trunk for severity. Tag: **D-D** (severity transfer source), with a cross-link to **D-B** (MTL emotion↔severity sharing).

5. **Robustness to erroneous ASR via audio+text fusion (D-D / voice pipeline).** [≈, Table 3] ASR-text alone is worse than oracle (0.599 vs 0.635), but `Cat{RoBERTaHyp, audio-rep}` (0.648) beats oracle text — i.e., the audio embedding *covers for* ASR errors. For Pebble's voice-message pipeline (audio → ASR → text head), this says: **do not throw away the audio embedding after transcribing**; fuse it with the text head so a 40%-WER child-speech transcript doesn't sink the prediction. Tag: **D-D** (transfer/feature design for severity on voice), **D-H** (pipeline).

6. **Multi-seed F1-max/avg/std reporting on tiny sets (D-C / D-D evaluation).** [≈, §2.2] Every result is mean ± std over **20 seeds**. On a ~35-example eval set, single-seed F1 is meaningless. Pebble's C-SSRS severity head (test set ~100 rows) is in the same regime — adopt the same multi-seed report. Tag: **D-C** (severity eval protocol), **D-D**.

### How each part helps Pebble succeed

- **(1)+(2) → the voice-message depression/severity head, concretely.** Build the audio severity head as: frozen **WavLM-base** backbone → extract **block ~8** (sweep 6/8/10) → temporal average pool per utterance → small Transformer/MLP head. Do NOT default to the last hidden state. Run the exact layer sweep on a child-speech dev slice; the inverted-U location may shift for children, but the *method* (sweep, pick the middle-meaning layer) transfers. Concrete artifact: a `voice/backbone_layer_sweep` experiment producing an F1-avg-vs-block curve like Fig. 2 before any head is frozen.
- **(3) → severity-head imbalance config.** Pebble's severity/depression labels will be skewed (most child messages are low-distress). Add **resampling-based augmentation** as a first-class config knob next to loss weighting: oversample the rare high-severity class to balance, and report F1-std across seeds to show the augmentation *stabilizes* not just shifts the mean. The `M- = N+·M+/N-` balancing rule is directly reusable for any segment/clip-resampling augmentation.
- **(4) → emotion-then-severity warm-start.** This is the empirical license for Pebble's MTL design where `emotion` and `severity` share a trunk: AER fine-tuning measurably lifted SDD. Concretely, for the voice head, pretrain/fine-tune the backbone (or an adapter) on emotion (MSP-Podcast / IEMOCAP) *before* the severity head, and ablate emotion-init vs cold-init. On the **text** side this is the analog of D-D's domain-adapted init for the severity head.
- **(5) → the voice pipeline must fuse, not cascade.** Architect the voice path so the WavLM audio embedding and the ASR-text NeoBERT embedding are **concatenated into the severity head**, not a cascade where text is the only survivor. Child ASR error rates will be high (accent, immaturity, noise) — this fusion is the documented mitigation that turned a 40.9%-WER transcript into above-oracle performance.
- **(6) → severity eval gate.** Make `severity` / depression-proxy evaluation report **F1/Pearson mean ± std over ≥20 seeds** on the small C-SSRS-style test set, so the D-C accuracy/wF1/macro-recall bars are reported with their seed variance, not a single lucky run.

### Child mental-health lens

- **Transfer validity — backbone & layer choice: moderate-to-high.** The *acoustic-linguistic hierarchy* (shallow=acoustic, middle=word-meaning, deep=identity) is a property of the SSL pretraining, not of the speaker population, so "use a middle layer" should transfer to child speech. But the **exact peak layer may shift**: children's speech has higher pitch, more dysfluency, different formants; the model's per-layer information profile on child audio is unmeasured. Mitigation: re-run the block sweep on child speech, never hard-code block 8.
- **Transfer validity — absolute F1 and the augmentation amount: low.** 0.89 is on **adult clinical interviews** with a trained virtual interviewer, session-level binary labels, 189 samples. Pebble scores **turn-level, mid-conversation, child** voice messages — a different unit, register, and label. The M+=500/1000 augmentation sweet spot is tuned to DAIC-WOZ's 189 sessions and will not transfer numerically. Treat all numbers as *relative ranking evidence*, not bars.
- **Transfer validity — emotion↔depression sharing: moderate.** "Emotion and depression share para-linguistic indicators" was shown on adult MSP-Podcast. Children express distress differently (less lexical, more behavioral/indirect), so the *shared trunk* idea transfers but the *specific MSP-Podcast init* may carry adult-emotion bias into a child model. Mitigation: prefer child- or youth-skewed emotion data for the warm-start if available; otherwise treat the emotion init as a regularizer to be validated, not assumed.
- **Risk — ASR domain shift is worse for children.** DAIC-WOZ WER was already 40.9% for adults with this ASR; child ASR is materially worse. The fusion mitigation (keep the audio embedding) is *more* important for Pebble, not less. But it also means the text-side severity signal is least reliable exactly on the population Pebble serves — another reason the voice path cannot be text-only.
- **Ethics.** Voice is biometric and identifying in a way text is not; a child's recorded voice plus a depression score is highly sensitive. DAIC-WOZ is a gated research corpus collected under consent; Pebble's child voice data needs stricter handling — on-device/transient processing of raw audio where possible, store embeddings not waveforms, explicit guardian consent, and never surface a raw "depressed/not-depressed" label to the child. The model informs the Decision Engine, it does not diagnose.

### Limitations & open questions for Pebble

- **Contradiction/gap vs Pebble's plan — no learned safety/depression head in v1.** This paper's entire value is a *learned depression detector from audio*. Pebble v1 trains only `emotion` and `severity` text heads; `safetyFlag` is heuristic and there is **no learned safety head in v1**, and the voice modality is not in v1's trained heads at all. So this paper is a **v2 blueprint**, not a v1 input — adopting its recipe means committing to the voice-message head that v1 explicitly defers. Surface this gap: the paper's strongest result (audio SOTA) targets a Pebble component that does not yet exist.
- **Contradiction/gap vs the text-encoder thesis (D-A).** Pebble's D-A is about *text* encoders (NeoBERT vs ModernBERT vs MentalBERT). This paper says, for the *voice* modality, the best text contribution comes from **RoBERTa-base over ASR transcripts fused with audio** — i.e., on voice, a generic RoBERTa + audio beats text alone, and a mental-health-specialized text encoder was never tested here. Open question: does a mental-health text encoder (MentalBERT) on noisy child-ASR even help, or does ASR error wash out its advantage? Untested.
- **Session-level vs turn-level.** The whole pipeline pools a 50-minute interview into one label. Pebble needs a score *per short voice message*. Sub-dialogue shuffling actually demonstrates the model can work on *sub-spans*, which is encouraging, but the minimum reliable span length for a depression signal is not characterized — a real open question for turn-level scoring.
- **Ensemble cost unaddressed.** SOTA 0.89 needs a **voting ensemble of three foundation models** (Ensemble 2 = WavLM + WavLMAER + RoBERTa over ASR). That is three ~95M backbones plus ASR plus RoBERTa at inference — far too heavy for an on-device/low-latency child app. Pebble would need to pick the single best fused system (`Cat{WavLMAER, RoBERTaHyp}`, F1-avg 0.756) and accept the ~0.13 F1-avg drop from the ensemble, or distill. The paper gives no latency/size numbers.
- **No calibration.** Like FAIIR (paper 01), this paper reports only F1, never calibration/ECE — yet Pebble's Decision Engine consumes probabilities. The calibration of an audio depression head on child voice is entirely unmeasured.
- **Reproducibility detail missing for fusion.** "Concatenation" of speech and text embeddings is stated but the projection/normalization before the head is not specified; Pebble must define this when building the fused severity head.
